# 🚨 First hour after a theft

Stay calm and work the list in order. Speed matters more than perfection.

> ⚠️ Anyone who DMs you offering to "recover" your funds for a fee is almost certainly a second scam. The Inquisition never promises recovery.

## 1. Stop further loss (minutes 0–15)
- Assume the wallet and its seed phrase are **compromised**. Do not send new funds to it.
- From a **clean device**, create a new wallet and move any remaining assets there.
- Revoke all approvals on the compromised wallet (only if it still holds something worth protecting — revoking costs gas).
- If you suspect your device: disconnect it, do not sign anything else on it.

## 2. Preserve evidence (minutes 15–40)
- Copy the **transaction hashes** of every outgoing theft transaction.
- Note the **attacker addresses** that received the funds.
- Screenshot: wallet history, the site or message that led to the incident, any signature prompt you remember, timestamps.
- Write down, while you remember: what you did in the hour before, which site, which link, which device.

## 3. Alert exchanges (minutes 40–60)
- Trace where funds went (block explorer; tools like Arkham or Breadcrumbs).
- If funds reached a **centralized exchange**, contact its security / law-enforcement team — not general chat support. Give: tx hashes, deposit address, time, and a short factual summary.
- Ask them to **flag or freeze** the receiving account.

## 4. Report
- File a police report — exchanges often need a case number to act.
- US: [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov). EU/UK/other: your national cybercrime unit.
- Report the attacker addresses to scam trackers (e.g. Chainabuse).
